Greek Chicken
Bowls

Charred corn, roasted zucchini, and quick-pickled onions over lemon couscous with baba ganoush — finished with a bright oregano vinaigrette. Built like the bowls at the fast-casual Mediterranean spots we love, sized for our table.

Serves3
Active40 min
HeatMild

This one's an homage to the build-your-own Mediterranean bowl spots — layered, colorful, a little indulgent, but built entirely from things you can pull off in a weeknight window. The vinaigrette and quick pickle do the heavy lifting; everything else is straightforward roasting and searing.

                Method

                10 steps
                1. Do first — 35–45 min, oven

                  Roast the eggplant

                  Preheat oven to 450°F. Prick the eggplant a few times with a fork and roast on a foil-lined sheet until the skin is deeply blistered and the eggplant has collapsed and feels totally soft, 35–45 minutes, turning once halfway.

                2. While the eggplant roasts

                  Quick-pickle the onions

                  Combine the sliced red onion, red wine vinegar, warm water, sugar, and salt in a jar. Stir until the sugar dissolves and let sit at room temperature while you cook — at least 20 minutes, longer is better.

                3. 15 min – overnight

                  Marinate the chicken

                  Whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, oregano, salt, and pepper. Add the chicken and toss to coat. Marinate at least 15 minutes, or up to overnight in the fridge.

                4. 10 min, once eggplant is cool

                  Drain and mash the baba ganoush

                  Let the roasted eggplant cool until safe to handle, then scoop the flesh into a strainer and drain 10–15 minutes, pressing gently. Transfer to a bowl and mash with the tahini, lemon juice, garlic, olive oil, salt, and cumin. Taste and adjust.

                5. Can do anytime before serving

                  Whisk the vinaigrette

                  Whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice, oregano, honey, and salt until emulsified. Set aside — it'll only get better as it sits.

                6. ~20 min, oven

                  Roast the zucchini

                  Preheat oven to 425°F. Toss the zucchini with a drizzle of olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread on a baking sheet and roast until tender and lightly browned, flipping halfway.

                7. 5–7 min, stovetop

                  Char the corn

                  Heat a dry skillet or grill pan over medium-high heat. Add the corn kernels and cook, stirring occasionally, until charred in spots.

                8. 10–12 min, stovetop

                  Cook the chicken

                  Heat a skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the marinated chicken until browned and cooked through (internal temp 165°F), about 5–6 minutes per side depending on thickness. Let rest 5 minutes, then slice.

                9. 5 min, off heat

                  Make the couscous

                  Bring the broth to a boil. Stir in the dry couscous, cover, remove from heat, and let sit until the liquid is absorbed. Fluff with a fork.

                10. Bring it together

                  Assemble the bowls

                  Divide couscous among bowls. Top with sliced chicken, roasted zucchini, charred corn, a scoop of your baba ganoush, and a handful of pickled onions. Drizzle generously with the vinaigrette and finish with feta and parsley.

                Notes from the Kitchen

                For the toddler

                Keep it simple

                Plate a portion of plain couscous, chicken, and zucchini before the vinaigrette and pickled onions go on — the base recipe has no real heat, so no other adjustment is needed. Go light on the raw garlic in the baba ganoush if it seems sharp for a toddler palate.

                Swaps

                Flexible base

                Swap the couscous for rice or quinoa if you prefer, and grill the chicken outside instead of pan-searing if the weather's nice.

                Storage

                Meal-prep friendly

                Leftover components keep well separately in the fridge for 3–4 days, so this is a great meal-prep bowl — pickled onions and vinaigrette both improve overnight.
