Chile-Braised Barbacoa

Chuck roast gone soft and dark in a covered pot, built on dried New Mexico chiles and a low oven that does all the work.

Hands-on 35 min
In the oven 3½–4 hrs
Feeds 3
Heat Mild · adjustable

The chuck doesn't need much from you — just dried chiles, an onion, and a few hours of patience while the oven turns a tough cut into something you can pull apart with a spoon.

        How it comes together

        Method

        One pot, oven set low. The only real technique is browning the beef well and not rushing the braise.

        Heat the oven, prep the chuck

        10 minutes

        Heat the oven to 300°F. Pat the chuck dry and cut it into large 2–3 inch chunks — bigger pieces stay juicier over a long braise. Season all over with salt and pepper.

        Toast and soak the dried chiles

        10 minutes

        Tear open the dried New Mexico chiles, discard stems and most seeds. Toast them in the dry Dutch oven over medium heat for about 30 seconds a side, until fragrant — don't let them scorch or they turn bitter.

        Pull them out, cover with about a cup of just-boiled water, and let them soften while you brown the beef.

        Brown the beef

        12 minutes

        Add a little oil to the Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown the chuck in batches on at least two sides, building a deep crust. Don't crowd the pot. Set the beef aside.

        Build the base

        6 minutes

        Lower the heat to medium. Add the chopped onion to the drippings and cook until soft and golden, scraping up the browned bits. Stir in the garlic and cumin for the last minute.

        Make the chile sauce

        5 minutes

        Blend the soaked chiles (plus their soaking liquid), the chipotle in adobo, the cooked onion, oregano, vinegar, and broth into a smooth, brick-red sauce. Taste — this is your moment to dial heat up or down.

        Braise low and slow

        3½–4 hours

        Return the beef to the pot, pour the sauce over, and tuck in the bay leaves. The liquid should come about halfway up the meat — add a splash of broth if needed. Bring to a bare simmer, cover, and slide into the oven.

        Braise until the beef shreds with no resistance, about 3½ to 4 hours. Check once at the halfway mark and nudge the heat to 275°F if it's bubbling hard.

        Shred and finish

        10 minutes

        Lift the beef out and shred with two forks. Skim excess fat from the sauce, then return the meat and toss so every strand soaks up the chile braise. Adjust salt, brighten with a squeeze of lime, and serve.

        Making it more El Paso

        How to take it from mild to classic

        As written

        One dried New Mexico chile and a single chipotle give warmth and smoke without real burn — toddler-friendly. The flavor is all chile, not heat.

        A step up

        Use two chipotles and a spoonful of their adobo. Leave some seeds in the New Mexico chiles. This is the everyday El Paso table version — warm, smoky, with a slow build.

        Border hot

        Add a dried chile de árbol or two to the toast-and-soak step, or a fresh serrano with the onion. Finish with a pinch of cayenne. Now it bites back the way the carnicería version does.

        To round out the plate

        What to serve it with

        Barbacoa is the centerpiece — keep the sides simple and let the meat lead.

        Warm corn tortillas

        The default. Double them up, pile on the beef, add raw onion and cilantro. A squeeze of lime over the top.

        Rice & charro beans

        Mexican red rice and brothy pinto beans turn it into a full borderland plate — the way you'd get it at an El Paso sit-down spot.

        Pickled red onions

        Quick-pickled in lime and vinegar, they cut the richness and add a bright snap against the deep chile.

        Crema & queso fresco

        A drizzle of crema and a crumble of queso fresco cool the toddler's plate and round out the adults'.

        Good barbacoa is mostly patience — the chiles and the oven do the cooking, you just have to wait.
